Quince paste Draft

  • Preserve
  • Autumn

Quinces are hard and sour raw, and turn rose-red and fragrant when cooked long. In Spain the paste is membrillo, eaten with Manchego; in Italy it is cotognata, cut into squares and rolled in sugar.

Quince seeds hold amygdalin. Cut out every core before cooking.

What you need

IngredientAmountNote
Quinces1 kgRipe and yellow
SugarAbout 800 gWeighed against the purée
LemonOneJuiced

The method

  1. Wipe the down off the quinces, quarter them and cut out the cores.
  2. Cover with water and simmer until soft, about forty minutes.
  3. Drain, push through a sieve, and add four fifths of the purée's weight in sugar.
  4. Cook on a low heat, stirring, until it pulls away from the pan.
When is it done?

A spoon drawn across the bottom leaves a path that stays open. It takes an hour or more.
