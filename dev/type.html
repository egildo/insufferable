Quince paste

Quinces are hard and sour raw, and turn rose-red and fragrant when cooked long. In Spain the paste is membrillo, eaten with Manchego; in Italy it is cotognata, cut into squares and rolled in sugar. See the method below.

What you need

  • 1 kg of quinces, ripe and yellow
  • About 800 g of sugar, weighed later
  • One lemon, juiced

The method

Cook the fruit

Wipe the down off the quinces, quarter them and cut out the cores. Cover with water and simmer until soft, about forty minutes.

Make the paste

  1. Drain the fruit and push it through a sieve.
  2. Weigh the purée, and add four fifths of its weight in sugar.
  3. Cook it on a low heat, stirring, until it pulls away from the pan.

When is it done?

A spoon drawn across the bottom leaves a path that stays open. It takes an hour or more, and the colour deepens all the while.

The quince is the most fragrant of fruits, and the least willing to be eaten raw.

Wrapped in baking paper, the paste keeps for a year. Press Ctrl+P to print the recipe.
